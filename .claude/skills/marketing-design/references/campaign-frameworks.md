# Campaign Frameworks — Reference

Longer-form strategy notes for `design-strategist`. Loaded on demand, not part of the main skill body.

## Funnel stage → creative intent

- **Cold / top-of-funnel** — pure hook-and-entertain or hook-and-educate; CTA is soft (follow, save)
  rather than a hard sell. Optimize for watch-through and shares.
- **Warm / retargeting** — can spend more time on objection-handling and proof; CTA can be direct
  (link in bio, promo code).
- **Hot / conversion** — lead with the offer, keep the hook short, spend the middle on urgency/proof,
  CTA is explicit and repeated (verbally and on-screen).

## Hook archetypes

Cross-reference against what the research actually found works for this specific account — don't
default to one archetype just because it's a favorite:

- **Curiosity gap** — states a surprising claim or result, withholds the "how" until later.
- **Pattern interrupt** — visually unexpected first frame (unusual angle, motion, color) that breaks
  feed scroll momentum.
- **Direct question** — addresses the viewer's specific situation/pain point in the first line.
- **Cold open mid-action** — starts mid-sentence or mid-action, implying the viewer already missed
  something.
- **Bold/contrarian statement** — challenges a common assumption in the niche.

## Objection-handling patterns

- **Social proof** — testimonial quote, review screenshot, follower/customer count.
- **Before/after** — visual or numeric comparison.
- **Demonstration** — show the product/service actually working, not just claims about it.
- **Authority** — credentials, press mentions, notable clients.
- **Risk reversal** — guarantee, free trial, low-commitment ask.

## Pacing notes

- Mobile short-form attention drops fast after any dead frame (no motion, no new information for
  more than ~1-1.5s). Every scene needs either camera/element motion or new information arriving.
- Reserve big transition moments (shader transitions in HyperFrames) for true beat changes —
  overusing them dilutes their impact and reads as try-hard rather than polished.
